The problem

Allergen risk lives in the changeover.

Most allergen controls are designed product by product. The risk appears when products share a line: a hazelnut brownie at 06:00, a plain loaf at 08:00, and a cleaning record that may or may not say what was done in between.

  • Undeclared allergens are one of the most common reasons for food product recalls.
  • On shared lines, the risk sits in the changeover: a hazelnut product followed by a nut-free one needs a validated allergen clean in between.
  • Allergen data is scattered across supplier specs, recipe sheets, schedules and paper cleaning logs, so checks are manual and happen under time pressure.
  • When a schedule changes mid-week, nobody re-checks every downstream changeover.

How it works

Start with documents you already have.

No sensors, no factory hardware, no MES integration to begin with. SavorCline works from specifications, schedules and cleaning logs, and people make the final call.

  1. 01

    Add products and specs

    Upload or paste supplier specifications and recipe sheets. Allergens are extracted, including hidden sources such as whey, spelt or tahini, and shown for review.

  2. 02

    Confirm allergen profiles

    A QA team member confirms each product's "contains" and "may contain" allergens. Confirmed profiles become the reference for every check.

  3. 03

    Check the week's schedule

    Every changeover on every line is assessed against the cleaning records. Risky transitions are ranked high, medium or controlled.

  4. 04

    Approve and report

    Accept or reject the suggested run order, then generate a dated report with findings, required cleans and a sign-off block.

AI, used carefully

The rules decide. The AI reads documents and explains.

Risk findings come from deterministic rules you can trace. AI is used to read supplier documents and to explain findings in plain language, and a person confirms everything before it is used.

What the AI does

  • Reads ingredient lists and specifications, including scanned PDFs (planned, with Amazon Textract)
  • Maps ingredients such as whey, casein or spelt to the allergen they contain
  • Explains a finding in plain language and lists the records it used
  • Suggests allergen profiles for a QA person to confirm

What it never does

  • Approve a run order or sign off a clean
  • Decide the risk level: the rules do, and show their working
  • Fill in a missing cleaning record or swab result
  • Use one customer's documents to answer another's questions

Safeguards

Built for QA teams that have to sign the report.

Every finding is traceable

Technology

How We Use Amazon Web Services

SavorCline's production platform is designed as a serverless pipeline on AWS: documents go into encrypted storage, Amazon Textract and Amazon Bedrock turn them into allergen profiles for a person to confirm, and AWS Lambda runs the changeover rules against each schedule. Today the risk engine runs in the browser in the live demo.

  1. 01

    Data collection

    QA teams upload supplier specifications, schedules and cleaning logs through an authenticated API.

    Amazon API GatewayAmazon S3
  2. 02

    Secure storage

    Documents encrypted with per-tenant keys; allergen profiles, schedules and findings stored per site.

    Amazon S3AWS KMSAmazon DynamoDB
  3. 03

    Processing

    Orchestrates upload → extract → interpret → assess, and runs the changeover rules.

    AWS Step FunctionsAWS Lambda
  4. 04

    AI intelligence

    Reads documents and suggests allergen profiles for review; explains findings in plain language.

    Amazon TextractAmazon Bedrock
  5. 05

    Application experience

    Sign-in with planner, QA and auditor roles; schedules, approvals and reports in the web app.

    Amazon CognitoAmazon API Gateway

These services are proposed, not yet deployed. The live demo runs in your browser on sample data. The full architecture →

  • Amazon Textract: Extract text and tables from supplier specifications and labels.
  • Amazon Bedrock: Interpret extracted text into allergen profiles; explain risk findings in plain language.
  • Amazon S3: Encrypted storage for uploaded specifications, schedules and cleaning records.
  • AWS Lambda: Document processing, risk evaluation and report generation.
  • AWS Step Functions: Orchestrate the upload → extract → interpret → assess pipeline.
  • Amazon DynamoDB: Ingredient profiles, allergen records, schedules and findings per tenant.
  • Amazon API Gateway: Authenticated API for the web app and future integrations.
  • Amazon Cognito: User sign-in and per-site roles (planner, QA, auditor).
  • AWS KMS: Per-tenant encryption keys for commercially sensitive supplier data.

FAQ

Questions QA teams ask first.

Email the founder
Is SavorCline available now?

SavorCline is building its initial product. The live demo on this site is fully interactive and runs on sample data or data you enter, stored only in your browser. Document upload and the AWS-hosted platform are in development, and early-access requests are open.

Does SavorCline replace our HACCP plan or allergen risk assessment?

No. It applies your allergen data and cleaning rules to the schedule consistently and shows where they are not met. Your HACCP team still owns the controls, the validation of cleaning methods and the final decision on every run.

What does the AI do?

In the planned platform, Amazon Textract reads supplier documents and Amazon Bedrock suggests allergen profiles and explains findings in plain language. A person confirms every extracted allergen. Risk levels come from deterministic rules, not from the AI.

Does the AI decide the production order?

No. SavorCline suggests a run order that needs fewer allergen cleans. A planner or QA lead accepts or rejects it.

Who can change what?

Planners edit schedules, QA confirms allergen profiles and signs off reports, and auditors get read-only access. In the demo roles are not enforced; in production they are enforced on the server.

Which allergens are covered?

The demo uses the 14 allergens regulated under UK and EU food information law, including hidden sources such as whey (milk), spelt (gluten) and tahini (sesame). The list is configurable for other markets.

How much does it cost?

Proposed launch pricing starts at $249 a month per site. Final pricing is agreed with pilot sites, and no payment is taken today. See the Pricing page.

What data do we need to start?

A product list with allergen information (or the supplier specifications to extract it from), a production schedule per line, and your cleaning records for the same period.
